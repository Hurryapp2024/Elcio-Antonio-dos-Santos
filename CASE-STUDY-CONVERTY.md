# Converty
## Structuring market signals into qualified opportunities

**Focus:** Business development · Research design · Commercial workflows  
**Status:** Product in development. This case presents intended workflow and qualification logic.

## The business problem

Research can produce a large volume of information without a clear path to commercial action. A useful opportunity needs context: its source, relevance, freshness, business fit, and the next step worth taking.

Converty is being developed to connect market signals with account organization, qualification, and outreach planning.

## My contribution

I defined prospecting criteria, source-tracking requirements, account and contact organization, and follow-up workflows. I reviewed prototypes and refined the relationship between research findings and relevant offers.

## Qualification design sample

This table translates the product direction into a practical review structure. It is a design artifact, not an assertion of a deployed scoring engine.

| Review question | Information to retain | Commercial purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Where did the signal come from? | Source and reference | Make research traceable |
| Is it still relevant? | Event date and current status | Avoid stale opportunities |
| Does it fit the offer? | Business need and fit rationale | Focus effort |
| What is confirmed? | Verified facts and unresolved questions | Separate evidence from assumptions |
| Who owns the next step? | Assignee and planned follow-up | Prevent opportunities from being lost |

## Design choices

**Preserve source context.** An opportunity should remain connected to the information that motivated it.

**Qualify before prioritizing outreach.** Volume alone does not establish relevance or commercial value.

**Connect research to an offer.** Follow-up should have a clear reason grounded in the account's situation.

**Keep review in the process.** Drafting and organizing outreach is distinct from automatically sending a message.

## Illustrative workflow

A researcher identifies a business signal and records its source. The signal is reviewed for freshness and fit, associated with an account, and given either a follow-up action or a reason not to pursue it.

Before outreach, a reviewer checks the underlying facts, the proposed offer, and the intended recipient.

## Proposed acceptance criteria

- A reviewer can trace an opportunity to its source.
- The record distinguishes confirmed information from open questions.
- Qualification includes a business-fit rationale.
- A next action has a clear owner.
- A research record does not, by itself, trigger message sending.

These are proposed criteria; this case does not claim they have passed production testing.

## Measures to evaluate

Source coverage, proportion of records with a documented qualification decision, follow-up completion, and eventual qualified-opportunity conversion could inform evaluation. No performance results are claimed.

## What this work demonstrates

The ability to turn a business-development process into structured information requirements and reviewable decisions.
